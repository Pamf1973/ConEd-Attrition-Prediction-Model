The journey

From one signal to many — building the drop-off predictor.

LL97 looked like the trigger pushing customers off steam — penalties that start in 2024 and tighten hard in 2030. After working with David's team we landed somewhere else: LL97 is one signal in a much bigger picture. A pre-war co-op on the Upper East Side drifts for completely different reasons than a midtown office tower.

LL97 alone → Twelve features → Drop-off predictor

04 / 08
How it works
The model

Two layers of machine learning.

Layer 01
K-means clustering
Group all 1,210 steam customers into five building archetypes. Every building gets one tag.
Pre-War Active ~320
Mid-Size Post-War ~285
Pre-War Stable ~245
Large Commercial ~210
Low-Compliance Residential ~150
Layer 02
Gradient boosting + SHAP
12 features feed in per building. The model returns a risk score 0–100; SHAP surfaces the top 5 drivers, signed.
LL97 carbon exposure Energy Star score Building age HVAC permits Neighbor patterns Steam demand size + 6 more

A pre-screening: the system surfaces who to look at, the relationship manager decides who to call.
