The Score Cell: the atom everything else inherits from

One cell in the rankings table carries four claims at once: how the queue is ordered, what tier we can defend, where the number came from, and how fresh the signal is. If the cell tells the truth, the table tells the truth, the case file tells the truth, and the report the analyst hands a manager tells the truth. This page is the contract for that cell.

01 · Laws of the atom

Six rules, none optional

L1
The model score never wears a percent sign.
ml_risk is a ranking, not a probability. It renders as a portfolio percentile ("96th") in tabular monospace. The percent sign is reserved for measured quantities only, like a weather-normalized YoY change. This single rule kills the wall of "100% High" in the current build.
L2
Color belongs to the defensible claim.
The severity tick and tier word come from the diagnostic rule, the transparent layer aligned with ConEd's own methodology. The ML percentile orders the queue but never colors it. One ramp: green, amber, red, deep red. Nothing else on the workbench uses these hues.
L3
Divergence is marked, not hidden.
When the ML rank and the rule tier disagree, a marker (◇) sits between them. Disagreement between the two layers is real information the analyst should carry into the call, not noise to smooth over.
L4
Provenance is a versioned claim.
Every score carries a chip naming the model and its validation status: GBM v1 · UNVAL today. When ConEd billing data lands in Phase 2 and the model back-tests against the intake benchmark, the same chip slot reads GBM v2 · BT 74%. The verified future is designed now, so it ships without a redesign.
L5
Absence of signal is a designed state.
Roughly two thirds of the portfolio has no fresh normalized YoY delta. That is the majority case, so it cannot render as a broken dash. The freshness chip always names the vintage of the newest delta; a stale vintage gets a dashed, muted treatment that reads as "known, older" rather than "error."
L6
Precision is never faked.
Saturated top scores tie. Ties render as ties ("99th, tied w/ 41 others" in the case file), never disambiguated by decimal places the model cannot support. The cell shows the percentile; the tie count lives one click away in the inspector.
02 · Anatomy

Five parts, one glance

GBM v1 · Unval Δ '24 −34%
1 2 3 4 5
1

Severity tick

3px bar, color from the one ramp, driven by the rule tier. Critical widens to 5px. Uncertain renders dashed and neutral. This is the only place ramp color appears in a row.

2

ML percentile

The sequencing claim. Tabular monospace, semibold, no percent sign (L1). Answers "where in the queue," never "how likely."

3

Tier word

The defensible claim, from the transparent diagnostic rule. This is the word the analyst repeats to a manager. Divergence marker (◇) appears here when it disagrees with the percentile (L3).

4

Provenance chip

Model identity plus validation status (L4). Neutral, never ramp-colored. The Phase 2 upgrade path lives in this slot.

5

Freshness chip

Vintage and value of the newest weather-normalized delta. The percent sign is legal here because this is a measurement (L1). Stale vintages go dashed and muted (L5).

Dimensions. Workbench density: 196–232px wide, two rows, 44px tall. The same anatomy loosens for presentation mode; nothing is added or removed, only spacing and size change.

06 · Open questions before this ships
  • Coverage reconciliation. The brief says diagnostic_risk covers 100% of rows, but norm_delta_23_24 covers only 35%. If the rule tier for most buildings is computed on a '23 vintage, the tier word and the freshness chip must agree about that. Reconcile with Ismael before wiring the chip.
  • Divergence population. How many buildings are actually S2? If it's 5, the marker is a nice detail. If it's 300, divergence needs its own filter and probably its own Monday question.
  • Percentile vs. tie behavior. If a large block ties at the raw ceiling, "99th" repeats down the screen. Confirm the tie counts and decide whether the cell shows the tie inline above some threshold.
  • Chip vocabulary with David. "Unval" and "BT 74%" are our shorthand. Before demo day, sanity-check that ConEd reads these the way we intend, alongside the open LL97-framing question already in the client notes.
