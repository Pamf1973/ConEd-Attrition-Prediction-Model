The Reasoning Report: the case file, printed

This is the artifact David asked for by name: a per-building document that "clearly states how we came to the conclusion." Its reader is a manager or account executive who has never opened the dashboard, encountering it as a PDF in Outlook or stapled to a CRM note, possibly printed in grayscale. It is the report register of the same system: same numbers, same provenance, same laws, different rhythm.

01 · Laws of the report

Inherits L1 to L6 and H1 to H5. Adds five.

R1
Nothing appears here that isn't on the case file.
The report is a projection of the header, never a second source. Same values, same formatting rules, same provenance labels. When the analyst presents this while the dashboard is open behind them, every number matches to the digit.
R2
One page is the argument. Page two is exhibits only.
A CRM attachment gets a two-minute read. Finding, narrative, key exhibits, and signature fit page one. Anything that spills goes to an exhibits appendix, and the argument must survive without it.
R3
Grayscale-safe by construction.
Assume a black-and-white office printer. Severity is carried by the word and the weight; the ramp color is an enhancement, never the encoding. Driver direction uses filled-vs-outlined bars, not red and green.
R4
Caveats travel with claims.
The AUC sentence sits under the ranking it qualifies. The estimation asterisk sits on the SC class it marks. The data vintage sits in the finding band. A caveat in a footnote is a caveat the reader never sees; here, nothing defensible is separated from its qualifier.
R5
A human signs it.
The report ships with "Prepared by" and "Reviewed" fields and cannot be generated as final without review. The model drafts; the analyst owns. This is the single strongest defensibility move available, and it costs one line of UI.

Finding

200 East 42nd St. ranks 4th of 1,210A active steam customers on attrition risk, and meets all three conditions of the Critical queue state. The largest pressure is carbon-law exposure: at current emissions the building faces an estimated $1,190,650C annual LL97 penalty when the 2030 caps take effect, and steam accounts for 55%A of its reportable emissions, which makes leaving steam the owner's single largest lever against that penalty.

The demand signal corroborates the pressure. Weather-normalized steam use fell 66%B year over year, crossing the diagnostic rule's −30%C high-risk threshold, while the building's energy intensity of 54.7 kBtu/ft² runs +99% above the office median. No recent DOB conversion permits are on file, which suggests the drop reflects operational change or negotiation posture rather than a completed conversion. That is the window for outreach.

Recommended action: account-team conversation anchored on LL97 compliance planning and applicable efficiency programs, before a disconnect or conversion permit is filed.

This is a screening analysis combining three layers: a transparent weather-normalized diagnostic rule (Exhibit D), a deterministic LL97 penalty calculation (Exhibit C), and a gradient-boosting ranking model (Exhibit A) trained on 57 buildings with observed late-stage demand drops. The ML layer is unvalidated against actual disconnect records and is used to order the queue, not to assert probability. Public data only: LL84, LL97, PLUTO, DOB, ACRIS.

1

Header block

Report ID, generation date, data vintage, model version, and workflow status at generation. Two reports about the same building can differ; the metadata says why. The ID makes it referenceable in a CRM note ("see RR-2026-0187").

2

Finding band

The claim ledger from the case-file header, restyled for paper. Critical appears with its definition spelled out in the sub-line, because this reader has never seen the methodology page.

3

Narrative with lettered citations

The dotted-underline citations from the dashboard become superscript exhibit letters in print, since links die on paper. Every number resolves to an exhibit on the same page.

4

Exhibits, strongest claim most prominent

The LL97 arithmetic (Exhibit C) is deterministic and labeled "not a model output," because the strongest thing in this report is statute math, not the classifier. Exhibit D shows the rule's actual thresholds and names the divergence from ConEd's per-customer method honestly.

5

Signature block

Prepared-by and reviewed fields (R5). The disclaimer is one sentence of decision-support framing, not a paragraph of legal hedging. A signed screening analysis is defensible; an anonymous model printout is not.

03 · Register rules

What changed from the workbench, and what didn't

Same
The spine.
Numeric formatting, tabular monospace figures, percentile-never-percent, provenance labels, tier vocabulary, the Critical definition, driver ordering, and every actual value. The same three type families (Space Grotesk / Inter / IBM Plex Mono).
Different
The rhythm.
White stock, wider leading, ~66ch measure, serif-free but editorial spacing. Severity color desaturates to an accent on the word only, and every encoding survives grayscale. Interactive affordances (chips, ticks, segmented controls) flatten to typographic devices: letters, rules, weights.
Never
Report-only numbers.
No value may be computed for the report that doesn't exist on the case file (R1). If the report needs a number the dashboard lacks, the dashboard gets it first. This is the single rule that keeps the two registers one system.
04 · Open questions before this ships
  • Format preference is an open client question. The client notes already flag it: Outlook plain-text, HTML body, or PDF attachment? This spec assumes PDF-first with an HTML twin. Ask David before building the pipeline; the answer changes the engineering.
  • PDF generation mechanics. New backend scope: server-side render (Puppeteer against this HTML, heaviest but pixel-faithful) vs. a PDF library (lighter, second layout to maintain). Recommendation: Puppeteer against a print stylesheet of this exact page, one layout, two outputs. Needs Railway container size check.
  • Territory gating. Also already in the client notes: are reports visible to everyone at ConEd with access, or gated by account territory? Blocks nothing now, blocks distribution later.
  • Review enforcement. R5 says a report can't be final without review. Decide the mechanic: watermarked DRAFT until the analyst confirms, or a hard gate. Watermark is honest and cheap; recommend it.
  • Peer median provenance in Exhibit B. The peer line needs a defined cohort (same cluster? same use type + size band?). Whatever the answer, the chart caption must name it, and the same cohort definition must drive the case-file chart.
