The Case-File Header: the score cell at full width

The Building Case File replaces the 400px drawer. Its header is the same atom from Spec 1, expanded: every abbreviation in the table cell becomes a full claim with its evidence attached. This is the surface the analyst reads for 3 to 5 minutes before a call, and the source every generated report cites. If a number appears in the emailed report, it appears here first, formatted the same way.

01 · Laws of the header

Inherits L1 to L6. Adds five.

H1
A ledger, not a hero.
No risk ring, no 96px number. The header is a stack of claims, each with its evidence: queue position with ties, rule tier with its threshold math, data coverage with its vintage. The anti-reference's "Risk Score: 82%" ring is exactly what this law forbids.
H2
Every claim shows its math.
The rule tier shows the delta against the threshold it crossed. The percentile shows rank and tie count. The provenance line translates AUC into words a manager understands: "ranks a true churner above a non-churner about 65% of the time." Nothing asks to be taken on faith.
H3
Direction by position, not color.
SHAP driver bars diverge around a center axis: right pushes risk up, left pushes it down. Bars are neutral. The severity ramp stays reserved for the tier claim (L2), so a screen full of drivers never competes with the one color that means danger.
H4
Values over abstractions.
Every driver shows its real-world value with unit: dollars, MT CO₂e, a score out of 100. This generalizes the inverted log formatters already working in BuildingPanel. "log_ghg: 6.66" never reaches the analyst's eyes.
H5
Generated text is labeled, cited, and editable.
The narrative names its source and version, visually distinguishes itself from measured data (dashed frame), and every number inside it is a citation back to a value on this page. Drafted text is never visually identical to data. The analyst edits before anything leaves the building.

1

Identity row

Address in display type, the only display-type moment on the page. The meta line carries the estimation caveat inline (SC-5* est.), and the right block anchors the four numbers every conversation starts from. Account owner: unmapped is a designed slot for the territory mapping that's still an open client question.

2

Claim ledger

Three columns, three epistemically different claims (H2). The ML column carries the honest AUC translation in plain words. The rule column shows the threshold math and names the ConEd-aligned method, because that alignment is the tier's authority. The coverage column says what we can and can't see.

3

Driver band

Rank, plain-language feature, real-world value (H4), diverging bar (H3), signed contribution. Down-bars render at lower opacity so the up-pressure story reads first without color. Energy Star at 77 pushing risk up is exactly the counterintuitive case that justifies showing values, not just directions.

4

Narrative slot

Dashed frame, provenance line, review status (H5). Every number in the draft is a dotted-underline citation resolving to a claim above it, which is what makes the generated report defensible rather than decorative. This is the Superhuman move: the AI drafts inside the object, not in a sidecar chat.

5

Action bar

Workflow state as a segmented control, always visible, filterable from the table. The one orange in the entire view sits on the primary action, which is the artifact ConEd explicitly asked for. Status changes are the feedback loop: Confirmed and False positive become future training labels.

03 · Workflow state vocabulary

Six states, each with a consequence

UnreviewedDefault. Appears in the Monday queue until an analyst touches it.
In reviewAn analyst has the case file open or has claimed it. Prevents double work when the team grows.
ContactedOutreach happened. Timestamp + optional note. Suppresses re-alerting for a cooling-off window.
Confirmed at-riskThe customer conversation validated the signal. Becomes a positive training label for GBM v2.
False positiveThe conversation contradicted the signal. Becomes a negative training label, and the most valuable feedback the model can get.
DismissedNot worth outreach for a stated reason (e.g., already negotiating, known closure). Reason is required; dismissals without reasons rot.

Why this is in the header spec: status is part of the claim the header makes. "High risk, contacted 2026-06-30" and "High risk, unreviewed" are different Monday-morning facts, and the delta feed on the This Week landing is largely a query over these states.

04 · Open questions before this ships
  • Does "Critical" exist? The diagnostic rule yields High / Medium / Low / Uncertain. Spec 1 rendered a Critical state. Either define it explicitly (proposal: High tier + fresh delta + top-decile percentile, i.e., concordant S1) or drop the word. An undefined severity level is precisely the kind of thing David's honest-framing instinct will catch.
  • Write path for workflow state. Status, notes, and dismissal reasons need endpoints, persistence, and an audit trail. Currently the API is read-only plus watchlist. Scope with Pedro before promising the feedback loop in a demo.
  • Narrative generation mechanics. /api/explain exists; decide whether drafts persist per building, regenerate on data refresh, and how citation anchors survive edits. A cited draft that drifts from its source values is worse than no draft.
  • Account-owner slot. Territory mapping is an open question with David. The header reserves the slot and renders "unmapped" honestly until that answer arrives.
  • Tie display threshold. "Tied w/ 2" is informative; "tied w/ 41" is a model-saturation confession. Decide the copy above a tie-count threshold, and confirm with Ismael how bad saturation actually is at the top of the ranking.
